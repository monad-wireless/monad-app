Monad Scan is the field app of a doctoral study at the Faculty of Informatics and Information Technologies, Slovak University of Technology in Bratislava (FIIT STU).

Every engineer filters noise out. We measure it. You are the noise.

A body in the path of a Wi-Fi signal changes it, and small sensor boxes in the building measure that change many times a second. The study asks whether that is enough to count the people in a room without a camera. To find out, the sensors need ground truth: people who stand in a known place at a known time and say so.

WHAT YOU DO
- Walk a quest. Go to a marked spot in the FIIT building, scan the printed QR card, and hold still for a moment. Some quests are a fixed route. The treasure hunt gives you a new route every time.
- Check in where you sit. Scan the card at your desk, study as usual, and the app counts how long you stay.
- Count heads. Some quests ask you to count the people you can see. Your number checks what the sensors guessed.
- Collect points and watch your coverage map fill in, one marked spot at a time.

While a quest runs, your phone sends a short anonymous Bluetooth beep, so the boxes know someone is there.

WHAT THE APP DOES NOT DO
- It does not read your location. The app has no code that can read a position.
- It does not save or upload photos, video or audio. The camera reads QR codes and, on a measurement walk, follows the phone's own movement.
- It does not record Wi-Fi data. No phone can.

WHAT LEAVES YOUR PHONE
Which marked spots you scanned, when, how long you stayed, and any headcounts you typed in. It is stored under a random code, never your name or email. Crash reports go to Firebase Crashlytics.

WHO CAN USE IT
Access is by invitation, for student volunteers of the study. Quests work only on site at FIIT STU in Bratislava. Away from the building the quest list is empty, and that is expected.
